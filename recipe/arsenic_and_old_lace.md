excerpt: |
  Before it took on its theatrical moniker, this drink entered the cocktail canon in the 1910s under a far crisper name: the Attention Cocktail, often shortened simply to the Atty. Early printed manuals recorded it as a bracing, aromatically charged riff on the Martini, weaving a measure of dry vermouth together with absinthe and crème de violette to build an intricate, floral-anise bouquet over a dry gin backbone.
  
  The cocktail’s transformation into Arsenic and Old Lace arrived in print in 1941, within former Broadway producer Crosby Gaige’s Cocktail Guide and Ladies' Companion. The timing was hardly a coincidence. In January of that same year, Joseph Kesselring’s dark comedy Arsenic and Old Lace opened on Broadway, rapidly becoming a smash hit with its tale of two seemingly sweet, elderly sisters poisoning lonely gentlemen with elderberry wine laced with arsenic, strychnine, and "just a pinch of cyanide."
  
  Given Gaige’s deep professional ties to the New York theater scene, the drink’s grimly charming rebrand seemed tailor-made for the cultural moment. While it remains speculative whether Gaige himself christened the cocktail or merely captured Broadway barroom slang of the day, the pairing was inspired. The ethereal violet hue and delicate perfume suggest antique lace, while the ghostly prickle of absinthe delivers the poison.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Brand |
| -----: | ---------- | ----- |
| 2 oz | London Dry Gin | Beefeater London Dry Gin |
| 3/4 oz | Blanc Vermouth | Dolin Blanc Vermouth de Chambéry |
| 1/4 oz | Crème de Violette | Rothman & Winter Crème de Violette |
| 2 dashes | Absinthe | St. George Absinthe Verte |

## Notes

- Method: Stirred
- Pour: Julep Strain
- Glassware: Nick & Nora glass
- Garnish: lemon oil, lemon twist
- Special Prep: None
