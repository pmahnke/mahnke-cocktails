excerpt: |
  The French Martini helped ignite the flavored “’Tini” craze of the 1990s, emerging as the defining emblem of the era's preference for sweet, fruit-forward cocktails. While the drink first came to prominence in the late 1980s, it cemented its iconic status after debuting on the cocktail menu at the famous New York City restaurant, Balthazar. Its runaway success paved the way for a decade of brightly colored vodka drinks, such as the Appletini, served exclusively in the iconic V-shaped glass.
  
  The cocktail earns its “French” moniker entirely from the inclusion of Chambord, a black raspberry liqueur produced in France since the 17th century. When paired with a neutral vodka base, the Chambord delivers a rich, dark berry flavor that is perfectly balanced by the tartness of fresh pineapple juice. A vigorous, hard shake with plenty of ice is essential to the drink's presentation, as it aerates the pineapple juice to produce the cocktail's signature creamy mouthfeel and luxurious frothy head.

## Ingredients

| Amount | Ingredient | Brand |
| -----: | ---------- | ----- |
| 2 oz | Vodka | Vanilla Flavoured Vodka or any Vodka |
| 0.25 oz | Black Raspberry Liqueur | Chambord |
| 1.75 oz | Fresh Pineapple Juice |  |
| 1 barspoon | Fresh Lime Juice |  |

## Notes

- Method: Shaken
- Pour: Double Strain
- Glassware: 
  - Martini glass
- Garnish: None
- Special Prep: 
  - Add the vodka, Chambord and pineapple juice into a shaker with ice and shake until well-chilled.
  - Fine-strain into a chilled cocktail glass.
